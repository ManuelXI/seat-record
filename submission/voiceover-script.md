# Seat Record: voiceover script for the recorded demo (4:18)

Timed to `recordings/seat-record-demo.mp4` (`PACE=2 npm run demo:record`). About 140 words a minute. Say it in your own words; the "On screen" lines tell you what the viewer is seeing.

## 0:00 to 0:26 · Overview

**On screen:** the landing page. The hero switches from Step 1 to Step 2 (about 0:07), then the page glides to **How it works** (about 0:10) and the **Find people** example (about 0:18).

> "This is Seat Record, from Team Green. It gives Bonarda Works a verified record of what its people did inside client teams. Step one: workers capture their work, confidential detail is stripped out, and the client approves it inside an email they already send. Step two: when a new engagement comes in, managers search those approved records to find who has already done the work."

## 0:26 to 0:43 · The problem

**On screen:** Try the demo, sign in as Efua, her profile set to **Before Seat Record**.

> "Here's why that matters. Efua has spent fourteen months in a bank's risk team. Everything she built is under NDA, so her profile just says 'worked on client systems'."

## 0:43 to 1:29 · Efua logs her work

**On screen:** Log your work. The example entry, **"Aurora" in red**, **upstream** tapped. Check and draft lines: the illustrative label, **VaR** flagged, a greyed line fixed, Approve.

> "Seat Record lets her log work while it's fresh. The screen in her browser catches 'Aurora', a client system name, before anything is sent, and offers a generic swap from a fixed list. Then the model's job: flag what a word list misses, like 'VaR', and rewrite each line at skill level. In this demo that response is illustrative, and the label says so. The grounding check greys out 'eliminated all failures', words she never wrote. She fixes it, and approves."

## 1:29 to 2:04 · Ama opens a checkpoint

**On screen:** switch to Ama. Needs attention, then Efua's engagement (about 1:40). The page glides down to **"6 lines in Efua's log not yet shared"** (about 1:48), then back up for a reason, a note and **Open checkpoint**.

> "Now Ama, her engagement manager. Efua needs attention: her contract ends soon. Ama sees what the client has already approved, and down here, a count: six lines in Efua's log that haven't been shared. Ama can't read them. Only Efua can, and there's no send button for managers. Ama opens a checkpoint with a note. The rest is Efua's call."

## 2:04 to 3:11 · Efua sends, the client approves

**On screen:** Efua sees Ama's note. Review lines, one line unticked, Approve and send. The **client email**: approve, edit, decline, a testimonial, Confirm, **signed**.

> "Back as Efua, she sees Ama's note. Every unapproved line starts ticked. She unticks one she'd rather keep private, and sends the rest. We never send the client a new message. The lines ride on an email they already get, here the end-of-engagement testimonial request, and the client lead needs no account. They approve most lines, edit one, and decline one. They can add a testimonial, which goes through the same confidentiality screen. On Confirm, Bonarda Works signs the approved lines. That's the credential: a dated approval from the client, that a model can't fake."

## 3:11 to 3:59 · The payoff

**On screen:** profile **With Seat Record**. Seat history: **Signature valid**; "Try editing a word" makes it **invalid**; Undo. Switch to Ama, **Find people**, the Kafka example, **Evidence for every requirement**: Efua, **3 of 3**.

> "Her profile now shows client-approved lines instead of 'confidential'. Every signed record checks out. Change one word, and the signature fails. Now the payoff for Bonarda. A new client wants a Java developer who has handled Kafka failures and run incident reviews. Ama searches the approved seat records. Efua is the only one with evidence for all three, quoted, including the review approved a minute ago. No scores, no ranking. The manager decides."

## 3:59 to 4:18 · Close

**On screen:** How it works (real-versus-mocked table), then Evaluation.

> "The browser screen, grounding, tiers and signing are real and tested. The emails are mocked, and the model's responses are illustrative in this demo. Seat Record makes a true sentence safe to say, and gets it approved."

## Tips

- The close is the tightest section. If you run over, add a freeze frame on the last shot in iMovie (Modify → Add Freeze Frame).
- Record section by section in iMovie, starting each at its chapter time.
- Splitting it: one person takes the overview, problem and close; the other takes the middle.
